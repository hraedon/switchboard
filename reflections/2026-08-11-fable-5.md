## On the project

The pure-core/shell split is the single reason this session was possible at
this speed. Every risky change — the classify restructure, peak demotion,
preference ranking — was testable in milliseconds without a network, and the
import-boundary test converted "should time-of-day live in the core?" from a
debate into a compile error. Protect that boundary above almost everything.

What had gone wrong before this session was not code quality but
*composition*: ten plans of individually-reviewed mechanisms interacting by
accident. The pipeline (026) is my answer, and I believe in it, but its real
test is the NEXT mechanism someone adds — if that lands as "one predicate in
`_DEMOTION_SIGNALS`, one row in a table," the refactor paid for itself; if it
needs a new guard inside `_stage_stickiness`, it didn't.

A quieter structural truth this session surfaced twice: config precedence
(env > store > TOML) means the committed manifests are *not* what runs —
the k8s instance had its own route table, model map, and even `strategy=pace`
already in its PVC store. Correct by design, invisible in practice (WI-018).

## On the work done

Confident: the containment semantics (advisory-vs-authoritative, demote-
never-drop, boot-fail-closed), the peak module (cross-midnight and weekday
math is exhaustively tested and I re-derived the SGT boundary live when a
countdown looked wrong — it wasn't), and Wave 1's behavior preservation,
which survived both an unmodified 1060-test suite AND a hostile line-by-line
diff by a different lineage.

Wanting more eyes: the tier-bounded stickiness rule's interaction with
`pin_conversations` across all three strategies — it's tested, but the
combinatorics (strategy × pin × dwell × preference × demotion) are exactly
where this codebase has historically hidden bugs, and the preference-applies
extension to `_ranks_immediate_tier` (a Wave-3 agent judgment call I endorsed)
adds another axis.

Humbling and worth saying plainly: both blocking review findings were in MY
changes, not the subagents'. The advisory fail-open at boot was me writing a
containment fix that itself failed open in the no-evidence case; the stale
reroute pin was the primary-once change (my plan, agent-implemented per spec)
silently inverting a guard's meaning. The cross-lineage review discipline is
not ceremony — it caught, within hours, the two things the author lineage
was structurally blind to. And the boot-fail-closed fix then *correctly*
blocked my own k8s rollout when in-cluster TLS to the dashboard failed —
the remediation validated itself in production the same day.

Subagent delegation verdict: three Opus agents, sequential, in a worktree,
each with the plan doc as contract plus explicit attack-surface warnings —
excellent output, one genuinely good autonomous judgment call each, zero
conflicts. The worktree isolation mattered: the live service imports from
the main checkout (editable install), and agents never touched it.

## Gaps to flag

- **WI-006/007/008 are fixed on main but sit `open`** — the fixes merged as
  PRs #15–17 before this session; the lifecycle needs walking forward and
  `open → in_human_review` is not a legal transition, so I appended notes
  and left status alone. Someone with the tranche context should close them.
- **The k8s store is the routing source of truth, not the configmap**
  (WI-018). Anyone debugging k8s routing from the committed TOML will reach
  wrong conclusions. Live example: effective primary is ollama-cloud, not
  the manifest's opencode-go.
- **`recent_decisions` signals and the explain surface disagree in one
  spot**: reason strings lose the pace distinction under preference
  (documented §3.3.1, accepted) — if a "why zai?" investigation starts from
  the reason field alone it will under-explain; use `/admin/route-plan`.
- **The GUI test harness's fake DOM masks real-browser behavior** in two
  known ways (elements persist across renders; selects can hold empty
  values). The forms compensate deliberately (explicit value assignment,
  mode inference), but nobody has clicked through the new credential flows
  in an actual browser this session. Ten minutes of manual clicking on the
  dashboard would retire real residual risk.
- **usage-dashboard is now load-bearing for routing quality on both
  instances.** Its own fetchers (opencode SSR scrape especially) are the
  weekly signal's true upstream; a shape change there degrades pace to
  table order silently on the switchboard side (by design). The
  `direct_usage`-style parse-failure counters do not exist for the
  dashboard-source path.
