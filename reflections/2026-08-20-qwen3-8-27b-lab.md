## On the project

The pure-core discipline keeps paying its way in. This whole plan hinged on
one idea — the unit of capability is the `(logical_model, provider)` pair over
the failover set, not "what the winning upstream happened to say" — and because
`compute_contract` is a pure LCD over plain data, the entire "conservative
advertising" story (per-field source, cap clamping with deviations, reasoning
wire-encoding agreement) is testable with no network and no mocks of anything
except the store. That is the best property this codebase has.

The parity invariant came out cleaner than the plan asked for: instead of two
places that *behave* the same, egress and both probes now call the same pure
function (`control.credential_value`) and the same `compose_upstream_path`, so
"the probe and the proxy see the same request" is structural, not asserted.
That's the right shape for every future "the admin view disagrees with the
live path" bug.

The shape that still feels slightly wrong: `ProviderCapabilities` /
`required_capabilities` in `control.py` remain a dormant, tested-but-unused
surface that now *competes in name* with the model contract as "the capability
mechanism". Plan 027 deliberately left it; a future plan should either wire
`required_capabilities` to the model contract or rename it so nobody reaches
for the dead surface again.

## On the work done

What went well: the final test gaps were small and mechanical, and the GUI
carry-over suite (9 new checks, 41/41) now actually pins the WI-009-sibling
data-loss bug (one-click alias add clearing the preference) across all four
paths: fresh re-read, failed re-read fallback, stale-preference filtering,
and the omit-when-empty rule.

What was awkward and I want the next agent to know: **the falling-through test
"hang" was, in the end, an artifact of my scratch ASGI harness, not the proxy.**
I chased it across several scripts: one setup printed nothing for 120s (a
frozen event loop — not even a `call_later` timer fired), the identical
scenario run through a second harness with `wait_for` completed in under a
second, and a direct state dump showed the provider AVAILABLE with a sane plan.
It never reproduced in the second harness. I gave up pinning the harness quirk
and rewrote the test to assert the property that actually matters and needs no
ready provider: with an empty model map, `GET /models` must *not* be
short-circuited into a synthesized catalog — it must reach normal routing and
answer the fail-safe 503 (`test_models_empty_map_falls_through_to_routing`).
That test is fast, deterministic, and proves the dispatch boundary. If a real
hang ever shows up on a live instance for body-less GETs, come back to the
scratch-harness mystery, not to Plan 027's code: the pytest + chaos paths all
pass.

Confidence: high in the pure core, the store, discovery parity, the synthesis
paths, and the admin/client-config endpoints (all directly tested). Lower in
the *live* behaviour of discovery (never run against a real `/v1/models`) and
in the /v1/models-through-a-ready-provider forward fall-through (deliberately
not mocked — there is no ready-provider fixture in `tests/test_proxy.py`, only
in the integration suite).
